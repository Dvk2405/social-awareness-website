SHORT NOTES

1. Child Labour – Concept and Socio-Economic Implications

Child labour refers to the employment of children in work that deprives them of their childhood, education and proper development. It usually occurs in factories, agriculture, domestic work and small industries. Child labour is considered a serious social problem and a violation of children's rights.

The main reason for child labour is poverty and lack of education. Many families depend on the income earned by their children for survival. Illiteracy, unemployment of parents and lack of awareness also contribute to this issue.

Child labour has serious socio-economic effects. It prevents children from receiving education and developing their skills. This leads to unemployment and poverty in the future. It also affects the physical and mental health of children.

Therefore, child labour must be eliminated through strict laws, education for all children and awareness programs among society.

2. Women Empowerment and Its Importance

Women empowerment means giving women the power, rights and opportunities to make decisions in their personal, social and economic life. It aims to achieve gender equality and remove discrimination against women.

Empowered women contribute significantly to the development of society. Education, employment opportunities and equal rights help women become independent and confident.

Women empowerment leads to social and economic progress. When women participate in education, politics and business, they help in improving family welfare and national development.

Thus, women empowerment is essential for creating an equal, just and progressive society.

LONG ANSWERS

1. Causes and Consequences of Child Labour and Remedial Measures

Child labour is a serious social problem where children are forced to work instead of attending school. It affects their physical, mental and emotional development.

Causes of Child Labour

  1. Poverty – Poor families send children to work to support household income.
  2. Lack of education – Absence of schools or awareness about education.
  3. Unemployment of parents – Parents depend on their children's earnings.
  4. Social inequality – Poor sections of society suffer more from this problem.
  5. Demand for cheap labour – Employers prefer children because they are paid less.

Consequences of Child Labour

  1. Loss of education – Children remain illiterate and unskilled.
  2. Health problems – Hard work affects their physical and mental health.
  3. Poverty cycle – Lack of education keeps families poor for generations.
  4. Violation of human rights – Children are deprived of their basic rights.

Remedial Measures

  1. Strict enforcement of child labour laws.
  2. Free and compulsory education for all children.
  3. Awareness programs for parents and society.
  4. Financial support for poor families.
  5. Rehabilitation programs for rescued children.

Eliminating child labour is essential for the development of both children and society.

2. E-Waste – Problems and Sustainable Management

E-waste refers to discarded electrical and electronic devices such as computers, mobile phones, televisions and batteries. With rapid technological development, the amount of e-waste is increasing worldwide.

Problems Caused by E-Waste

  1. Toxic substances – E-waste contains harmful materials like lead, mercury and cadmium.
  2. Environmental pollution – Improper disposal contaminates soil, air and water.
  3. Health risks – Exposure to toxic chemicals can cause serious diseases.
  4. Loss of valuable resources – Metals like gold and copper are wasted.

Sustainable Management Strategies

  1. Recycling – Proper recycling helps recover useful materials.
  2. Reuse – Old electronic devices should be reused or refurbished.
  3. Awareness programs – People should be educated about proper disposal.
  4. Government regulations – Strict laws for e-waste management should be implemented.
  5. Producer responsibility – Companies should collect and recycle old devices.

Effective management of e-waste is necessary to protect the environment and human health.

3. Importance of Voting Rights and Human Rights in Democracy

Voting rights and human rights are essential elements of a democratic society. They ensure equality, freedom and participation of citizens in governance.

Importance of Voting Rights

Voting rights allow citizens to elect their representatives in government. Through voting, people can express their opinions and influence political decisions. It strengthens democracy by ensuring that leaders are chosen by the people.

Voting also promotes accountability. Elected representatives must work for public welfare because they depend on the support of voters.

Importance of Human Rights

Human rights are basic rights and freedoms that belong to every individual. These include the right to life, freedom of speech, education and equality before the law.

Human rights protect individuals from discrimination and injustice. They ensure dignity, freedom and equal opportunities for all people.

Conclusion

Both voting rights and human rights are necessary for a strong and fair democracy. They promote justice, equality and active participation of citizens in the development of society.
